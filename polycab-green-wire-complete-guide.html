What Is Polycab Green Wire?

Polycab Green Wire is a PVC-insulated, single-core house wire built around a bunched copper conductor. "Bunched" construction means the conductor is made from multiple fine copper strands twisted together rather than a single solid wire, which is what gives house wiring its flexibility during installation, especially when it has to be pulled through conduit or bent around switch boxes.

According to Polycab's official product information, the range uses HR FR-LSH LF insulation. Each part of that abbreviation describes a specific property:

  • HR (Heat Resistant): the insulation is designed to tolerate higher operating temperatures than basic PVC insulation.
  • FR (Flame Retardant): the insulation resists sustaining or spreading a flame once ignited.
  • LSH (Low Smoke Halogen-related): the formulation is designed to release comparatively low smoke, which matters for visibility and evacuation during a fire.
  • LF (Lead Free): the PVC compound does not use lead-based stabilisers.

Polycab's official listing states an operating temperature range of -15°C to 85°C for the 180-metre Green Wire range, and describes the insulation as offering meaningfully higher heat resistance than standard PVC house wire. This type of construction is aimed at homes, offices, and light industrial settings where both flexibility and a degree of fire safety are wanted from the wiring.

It is worth being precise here: "Green Wire" is a specific Polycab product line name, not simply any house wire that happens to be sold in a green colour. Polycab also sells its Etira, Primma, and Optima+ ranges in a green colour option for circuit identification, and those use different insulation specifications. If you are buying based on a colour match alone, check the product code and insulation type before assuming it is the Green Wire range.

Key Features of Polycab Green Wire

The features below are drawn from Polycab's official product pages for the Green Wire and Green Wire+ ranges. Where a feature is specific to one range rather than the other, that is noted.

  • 99.97% high-purity copper conductor: higher-purity copper generally offers better conductivity, which supports current-carrying performance and can help reduce resistive losses in the circuit.
  • Lead-free PVC insulation: the insulation compound is formulated without lead-based additives, which is relevant to environmental and health-conscious buyers.
  • Flame-retardant, low-smoke behaviour: in a fire, the insulation is designed to slow flame propagation and release less smoke than ordinary PVC, which can help with visibility during evacuation.
  • Heat resistance: Polycab states the Green Wire range offers around 20% higher heat resistance than standard PVC wiring (rated to approximately 80°C), while the Green Wire+ range is stated to offer around 30% higher heat resistance, rated to approximately 90°C. These are two different figures for two different products, so do not assume they are interchangeable.
  • High abrasion resistance: the outer insulation is built to resist surface wear from handling and installation.
  • Rodent protection: both ranges are described as offering resistance to rodent damage, which is relevant for concealed wiring runs.
  • RoHS and REACH compliance: these are chemical-safety and environmental compliance frameworks that restrict the use of certain hazardous substances in the product.
  • Moisture resistance: stated specifically for the Green Wire+ range on Polycab's official page.
  • GreenPro certification: Polycab describes Green Wire+ as India's first GreenPro-certified house wire range, a green-building product certification associated with IGBC (the Indian Green Building Council). This certification claim is specific to Green Wire+ and should not be assumed to apply to the base Green Wire range unless separately confirmed.

None of this should be read as a guarantee that the wire is suitable for every installation. Suitability still depends on the circuit design, the installation method, and compliance with the applicable wiring rules for the specific project.

Polycab Green Wire Sizes and Variants

Based on Polycab's current official product listings, the two related ranges are sold in different size sets and different coil lengths, which is one of the more practical distinctions buyers overlook.

Polycab Green Wire (180-metre coils): available in 1 sq.mm, 1.5 sq.mm, 2.5 sq.mm, 4 sq.mm, and 6 sq.mm conductor sizes, in black, blue, green, red, and yellow.

Polycab Green Wire+ (90-metre coils): available in a wider size range, including 0.75 sq.mm, 0.8 sq.mm, 1 sq.mm, 1.5 sq.mm, 2.5 sq.mm, 4 sq.mm, and 6 sq.mm.

Colour availability can vary by size and by seller, so it should be confirmed on the current listing rather than assumed.

Conductor size should never be chosen by habit or by copying a neighbour's installation. The right size depends on:

  • the connected electrical load on that circuit
  • the type of circuit (lighting, socket, or a dedicated appliance circuit)
  • the installation method (in conduit, surface-mounted, or embedded)
  • the applicable electrical wiring standards for the installation
  • voltage drop over the length of the run
  • coordination with the protective device (MCB or fuse rating) feeding that circuit

A qualified electrician or electrical designer is best placed to translate these factors into a final size, particularly for anything beyond a simple single-point lighting circuit.

Polycab Green Wire Colours

Polycab's official listing for the 180-metre Green Wire range shows black, blue, green, red, and yellow as available colour options. In Indian residential and commercial wiring practice, using different colours for different circuits or phases is common because it makes future maintenance, fault-finding, and circuit identification considerably easier.

It is important to separate two different things here: the colour options a manufacturer offers for a particular wire, and the conductor identification or colour-coding conventions used in a specific electrical installation. The product's available colours are a manufacturing choice; how those colours are actually assigned to phase, neutral, and earth conductors on a project should follow the wiring design and the applicable standards for that installation, not simply whichever colour happens to be in stock. If you are unsure how colour-coding should be applied on your project, that is a question for the electrician or electrical consultant handling the design.

How to Choose the Right Polycab Green Wire Size

Rather than pointing to one "correct" size, it helps to walk through the same process an electrician would use:

  1. Determine the connected load. Add up the wattage of everything that circuit will power, and convert that to current.
  2. Understand the circuit type. A lighting circuit, a general socket circuit, and a dedicated circuit for a single high-power appliance are usually sized differently.
  3. Consider the installation method. Wire run in open air behaves differently, thermally, than wire bunched inside conduit or embedded in a wall, and this affects the safe current-carrying capacity.
  4. Check the current-carrying capacity of the conductor size you are considering, based on the manufacturer's specification and the applicable wiring standard.
  5. Account for voltage drop, particularly on longer cable runs, since excessive voltage drop affects equipment performance even if the wire is technically rated for the load.
  6. Match the protective device. The MCB or fuse protecting the circuit needs to be coordinated with the conductor size, not chosen independently of it.
  7. Check the applicable Indian electrical wiring standards relevant to the type of installation.
  8. Confirm the manufacturer's current datasheet for the specific product code you are considering, since specifications can be updated.
  9. Consult a qualified electrician for the final decision, especially on anything beyond a simple, low-risk circuit.

This is a process, not a shortcut. Skipping steps to save time on sizing is one of the more common ways electrical installations end up underperforming or, in worse cases, becoming a safety risk.

What Affects the Price of Polycab Green Wire

A single number for polycab green wire price is not particularly useful, because the final figure depends on several variables at once, not just the conductor size.

The main factors that influence price include:

  • Conductor size — larger cross-sectional areas use more copper, which is the single biggest cost driver in any house wire.
  • Copper content and purity — higher-purity copper, like the 99.97% purity Polycab states for this range, affects material cost.
  • Coil length — a 90-metre coil and a 180-metre coil of the same size are not directly comparable on price per coil; price per metre is the fairer comparison.
  • Product series — Green Wire and Green Wire+ are different products with different specifications, so they are not necessarily priced the same even at matching sizes.
  • Insulation specification — HR FR-LSH LF insulation, being a more advanced formulation, typically costs more to produce than basic PVC or standard FR insulation.
  • Market conditions — copper is a globally traded commodity, so wire prices move with copper market pricing over time.
  • Applicable taxes, seller-level promotions, and order quantity also affect what a buyer ultimately pays.
  • Availability — a specific size, colour, or coil length being in or out of stock at a given time can affect the price shown.

Because of all this, online prices can and do change. If you want a current figure, check the live product listing rather than relying on a number quoted in an article, since displayed prices are updated more frequently than written content.

Understanding 90-Meter Coil Options

Coil length matters more than it might first appear, particularly for anyone planning a wiring project with a defined budget or a specific quantity requirement. Polycab Green Wire+ is sold in 90-metre coils across its available sizes, which sits alongside the 180-metre coil length used for the base Green Wire range.

A 90-metre coil can be a practical choice for smaller jobs, partial rewiring work, or situations where a contractor wants to avoid holding excess stock of a size that will not be fully used. For larger projects, a 180-metre coil (or multiple coils) may work out more efficient in terms of handling and per-metre cost, though this should be checked against the actual per-metre pricing rather than assumed.
