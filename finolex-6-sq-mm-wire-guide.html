If you are planning an electrical installation that involves a heavier load than typical lighting or socket points, you have probably come across the term “6 sq mm wire.” Wire size is one of the most important decisions in any electrical wiring project, because a conductor that is too small for a circuit can overheat, while an oversized conductor adds unnecessary cost and installation difficulty. This guide looks specifically at Finolex 6 sq mm wires — what they are, what “6 sq mm” actually means, the features you should know about, and the questions worth asking before you buy.

What Is a Finolex 6 Sq mm Wire?

A Finolex 6 sq mm wire is an electrical wire manufactured by Finolex Cables in which the conductor has a nominal cross-sectional area of 6 square millimetres. Finolex is one of India's established wire and cable manufacturers, and its house wire range includes several product lines — such as Flame Retardant (FR) Grade wires and Flame Retardant Low Smoke (FR-LSH) wires — each offered across a range of conductor sizes, including 6 sq mm.

“Sq mm,” short for square millimetres, refers to the cross-sectional area of the copper conductor inside the wire, not the overall diameter of the finished, insulated wire. This is a standard way of specifying wire size in India and is used consistently across the electrical industry, from 0.75 sq mm wires used for light fittings to larger sizes such as 4 sq mm and 6 sq mm used for higher-load circuits.

As a general principle, a conductor with a larger cross-sectional area can carry more current than a thinner conductor made of the same material, under comparable conditions. However, the actual permissible current for any specific wire depends on multiple factors — including the installation method, ambient temperature, cable length, grouping with other cables, and the requirements set out in the applicable Indian Standard. Because of this, a specific current rating should not be assumed without referring to the manufacturer's documentation and relevant standards for the exact installation conditions involved.

Buyers should understand that Finolex does not offer just one single “6 sq mm product.” Depending on the product line — FR Grade, FR-LSH, or other variants — insulation composition, packaging, and specific technical parameters can differ. Check the specific product listing or datasheet rather than assuming all Finolex 6 sq mm wires are identical.

Key Features of Finolex 6 Sq mm Wires

While exact specifications can vary by product line, several features are consistently associated with Finolex house wires, based on Finolex's own product documentation:

Conductor

Finolex house wires use copper conductors described by the company as being manufactured from high-purity electrolytic grade copper, with a minimum conductivity of 100% IACS (International Annealed Copper Standard) on Finolex's FR Grade product datasheet. This conductivity specification is associated with the FR Grade product line as documented by Finolex; buyers should verify the conductor specification for the exact product and size they intend to purchase.

Insulation

Finolex house wires are PVC insulated. The FR (Flame Retardant) grade insulation compound is designed to resist the spread of flame, while the FR-LSH (Flame Retardant Low Smoke Halogen-free) variant is designed to additionally reduce smoke and toxic gas emission in the event of fire, according to Finolex's own product categorisation. Whether a specific 6 sq mm product is FR or FR-LSH depends on which product line you select — this is a characteristic of that particular product, not a separate topic in itself.

Standards and Certification

Finolex's FR Grade house wires are stated by the manufacturer to be manufactured to be suitable up to 1.1 kV grade as per IS 694:2010, the Indian Standard covering PVC-insulated cables for working voltages up to and including 1100 V. Finolex house wires are also widely described as ISI marked, indicating conformity with relevant Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) requirements. Confirm the specific standard reference printed on the packaging or datasheet for the exact variant purchased.

Thermal and Fire-Related Properties

Finolex's technical documentation for its FR Grade product cites parameters such as thermal stability (minimum 150 minutes), volume resistivity (minimum 5.5×10¹³ Ω-cm/km), and an oxygen index of more than 29%, along with a temperature index of a minimum of 250°C at 21% oxygen concentration. These figures describe how the insulation compound is designed to behave under heat and fire-test conditions, based on Finolex's published specifications for this product line.

Packaging and Length

Finolex house wires, including several 6 sq mm products, are commonly available in standard coil lengths such as 90 metres, based on Finolex's own product listings. Other lengths may be available depending on the retailer and specific product variant, so confirm the coil length before purchase.

Available Variants

Finolex's house wire range for sizes such as 6 sq mm generally spans multiple product lines, including FR Grade (in different colour/packaging options), FR-LSH, and premium ranges such as FinoUltra. Each variant may differ in insulation compound, certification references, or intended use case, so it's worth checking which specific line a given 6 sq mm product belongs to.

Where Are Finolex 6 Sq mm Wires Used?

Wires of this size are generally associated with circuits that carry a comparatively higher electrical load than standard lighting points or 6A socket circuits. Based on general Finolex product positioning and accepted electrical practice, 6 sq mm wires may be considered for:

  • Higher-capacity domestic circuits, such as those feeding air conditioners, geysers, or other heavier appliances, subject to load calculations and local wiring rules
  • Distribution wiring between a meter, distribution board, or sub-panel
  • Commercial or light industrial wiring where the connected load justifies a larger conductor
  • General power circuits, as part of a wider wiring design

It's important to note that suitability for a specific appliance or circuit is not something a wire size alone can guarantee. The correct wire size for any circuit depends on the connected load, the length of the cable run, the method of installation, ambient conditions, voltage drop limits, and the protective devices (such as MCBs) used in the circuit. For this reason, wire selection for a specific installation should ideally be verified against relevant standards or confirmed with a qualified electrician, rather than decided on size alone.

Finolex 6 Sq mm Wire vs Other Wire Sizes

Wire sizing is not about one size being universally “better” than another — it is about matching the conductor to the electrical requirement of the specific circuit. Commonly used smaller sizes in domestic and light commercial wiring include 1 sq mm, 1.5 sq mm, 2.5 sq mm, and 4 sq mm, alongside 6 sq mm.

  • 1 sq mm and 1.5 sq mm wires are typically used for lighting circuits and low-load applications, where the connected load is relatively small.
  • 2.5 sq mm wire is commonly used for general socket outlets and moderate-load circuits.
  • 4 sq mm wire is often used where a higher load than a standard socket circuit is expected, such as for select appliance circuits.
  • 6 sq mm wire is generally positioned for higher-load circuits, sub-mains, or distribution wiring, compared to the smaller sizes above.

The right choice between these sizes depends on the actual electrical load, cable run length, voltage drop considerations, and the applicable wiring standards for your installation — not simply on picking the largest available size.
