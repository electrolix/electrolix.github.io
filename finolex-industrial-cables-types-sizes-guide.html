Practical tip: these are typical starting points, not fixed rules. Actual current-carrying capacity depends on ambient temperature, how the cable is laid (in conduit, on tray, or buried), and how many circuits run together. For critical or high-load installations, size the cable against the full load calculation rather than the nearest match in a table.

Installation and Safety Best Practices

Even the right cable can underperform if it's installed poorly. A few practices worth following on any industrial site:

  • Never bunch multiple loaded circuits tightly in one conduit without derating — heat builds up and shortens cable life
  • Support long horizontal or vertical runs with proper cable trays or cleats so insulation isn't stressed under its own weight
  • Label circuits at both ends during installation — this saves hours during future maintenance or fault-finding
  • Keep cable away from sharp edges, unprotected conduit ends, or direct contact with hot machinery surfaces
  • Test insulation resistance after installation and before energising, especially on new panel wiring

How to Choose the Right Finolex Industrial Cable

A quick checklist before you place an order:

  • Calculate the load (in amps) your circuit will carry, and match it to the correct sq mm rating
  • Choose FRLS or FRLSH insulation for enclosed or high-occupancy industrial areas
  • Factor in run length — longer distances may need a larger gauge to minimise voltage drop
  • Always verify ISI markings and buy from an authorised distributor to avoid counterfeit cable
  • Keep a small buffer stock of the same batch/size for future repairs or extensions
