What Is Finolex 0.75 Sq mm Wire?

Finolex 0.75 sq mm wire is a single-core, unsheathed, flexible copper wire with a nominal conductor cross-section of 0.75 square millimetres, insulated with PVC. It is manufactured by Finolex Cables Limited, one of India's established wire and cable manufacturers, and is sold under Finolex's House Wires range.

The conductor is made of multiple fine strands of bright annealed, electrolytic-grade bare copper, which keeps the wire flexible enough for conduit wiring and tight bends. According to Finolex's own technical literature for this product line, the copper conductor is at least 99.96% pure with a minimum conductivity of 100% IACS (International Annealed Copper Standard).

Finolex markets 0.75 sq mm wire under more than one product pack — for example, Flame Retardant (FR) “Silver” and “Gold” grade packs are both listed in this size. These packs may differ in packaging, positioning, or price, so it's worth checking the exact product name and code on the listing you intend to buy rather than assuming all 0.75 sq mm Finolex wires are identical. The wire is single-core and unsheathed, meaning each wire carries one conductor with PVC insulation only (no additional outer sheath), and it is rated for a voltage grade of up to 1100 V.

What Does 0.75 Sq mm Mean?

“0.75 sq mm” refers to the nominal cross-sectional area of the copper conductor inside the wire, measured in square millimetres. It is not the outer diameter of the finished, insulated wire, which is larger — Finolex's own data lists the overall diameter of the 0.75 sq mm FR wire as approximately 2.3 mm.

This cross-sectional area matters because it directly affects two things: how much current the wire can carry without overheating, and how much resistance (and therefore voltage drop) the wire has over a given length. A smaller cross-section, like 0.75 sq mm, carries less current and has higher resistance per metre than a larger size such as 1.5 sq mm or 2.5 sq mm. This is why wire size selection is based on the expected electrical load of the circuit, not just on availability or cost.

Applications and Suitable Uses

Finolex's own product listing describes this wire as suitable for “homes, buildings, industrial buildings, engineering, procurement & construction” applications generally, covering its FR PVC wire range in various sizes including 0.75 sq mm. Within that general description, the 0.75 sq mm size specifically — with its 6–7 A current rating — is best understood as a light-duty or control-wiring size rather than a wire meant for high-current sockets or heavy appliance circuits.

Typical uses reported for 0.75 sq mm PVC-insulated copper wire in Indian wiring practice include:

  • Control panel and control circuit wiring, where current draw is low
  • Internal wiring of light fixtures, switches, and indicator/pilot lamp circuits
  • Signal, bell, and low-current interconnections within a building
  • Short-run wiring inside distribution boards and switchgear enclosures

Because the current-carrying capacity of this size is limited compared with 1 sq mm and above, it is generally not the wire chosen for general-purpose power sockets, air conditioners, geysers, or other higher-load domestic appliances — those circuits usually call for 1.5 sq mm, 2.5 sq mm, or larger conductors, sized according to the connected load. Whether 0.75 sq mm wire is appropriate for a specific lighting or control application depends on several factors beyond conductor size alone: the actual connected load, the length of the cable run (which affects voltage drop), the ambient temperature and whether the wire is bunched with others, and the rating of the protective device (MCB/fuse) feeding the circuit. Because incorrect sizing can create a fire or safety hazard, it's advisable to have circuit design and wire selection confirmed by a qualified electrician, particularly for anything beyond simple, low-current wiring.

Finolex 0.75 Sq mm Wire vs Other Sizes

A common question buyers have is how 0.75 sq mm compares with the next sizes up — 1 sq mm and 1.5 sq mm. Using Finolex's own published figures for its FR PVC single-core cables, the comparison looks like this:

1.0 sq mm 2.5 mm 11 A 12 A 19.5 Ω
1.5 sq mm 2.7 mm 13 A 16 A 13.3 Ω

Two things stand out from this table. First, current-carrying capacity increases noticeably with each step up in conductor size — moving from 0.75 sq mm to 1 sq mm roughly doubles the conduit rating, because more copper cross-section allows more current to flow before the wire heats up beyond its rated limit. Second, conductor resistance drops as the size increases, which reduces voltage drop over longer cable runs.

This doesn't make the larger sizes automatically “better” — it makes them appropriate for different jobs. A 0.75 sq mm wire is lighter, marginally cheaper per metre, and easier to route in tight spaces, which suits low-current control or lighting-accessory wiring. A 1 sq mm or 1.5 sq mm wire is the more common choice where the circuit needs to carry a meaningful domestic load, such as general lighting circuits or plug points, and where Indian wiring practice typically specifies larger conductors for safety margin. The right choice depends on the calculated load of the circuit, not on which size happens to be cheaper.
