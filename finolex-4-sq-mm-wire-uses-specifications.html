What Is a Finolex 4 Sq mm Wire?

A Finolex 4 sq mm wire is a single-core, copper-conductor, PVC-insulated electrical wire manufactured by Finolex Cables, with a nominal conductor cross-sectional area of 4 square millimetres. It falls under Finolex's House Wires range, which is manufactured to be used in homes, buildings, and industrial or commercial premises.

The term "sq mm" (square millimetre) refers to the nominal cross-sectional area of the copper conductor — essentially, how thick the current-carrying copper core is, not the outer diameter of the finished wire with its insulation. A larger cross-sectional area generally allows a conductor to carry more current than a smaller one under comparable conditions. However, the actual permissible current for any installation depends on several factors, including the installation method, ambient temperature, cable length, grouping with other cables, and the requirements of the applicable wiring standard. A 4 sq mm wire is physically larger than common smaller sizes such as 1 sq mm, 1.5 sq mm, and 2.5 sq mm, but this does not automatically mean it is the correct choice for every circuit — the size still needs to match the load and wiring design of the specific circuit it is used in.

Before buying, it helps to understand that Finolex does not sell a single, uniform "4 sq mm wire." It offers this conductor size across more than one product line within its house wires range, and the exact construction, insulation grade, and certification details can differ between these product lines. The sections below walk through what is verified about these products.

Key Features of Finolex 4 Sq mm Wires

Finolex publishes technical details for its house wire range, including 4 sq mm variants, on its official product pages and datasheets. Based on this documentation, here are the verified characteristics that are consistently associated with Finolex's flame-retardant (FR) grade house wires in this size:

  • Conductor: Multiple strands of finely drawn, bright annealed, electrolytic-grade bare copper, with a stated purity of more than 99.97% and a minimum conductivity of 100% IACS (International Annealed Copper Standard).
  • Insulation: A flame-retardant (FR) PVC compound, formulated and manufactured in-house by Finolex, designed to resist the spread of fire along the cable.
  • Standard: Manufactured as FR-grade PVC insulated flexible cables up to 1.1 kV grade, in line with IS 694:2010, the Indian Standard covering PVC insulated cables for working voltages up to and including 1100 V.
  • Rated voltage: 1100 V, as specified on Finolex's official FR-grade house wire documentation.
  • Thermal stability: A minimum of 150 minutes, as per Finolex's published specification sheet for its FR-grade house wires.
  • Volume resistivity: A minimum of 5.5 × 10¹³ Ω-cm/km, indicating the insulation's resistance to electrical leakage.
  • Oxygen index: More than 29%, and a temperature index of a minimum of 250°C at 21% oxygen — both are indicators of the insulation's flame-retardant performance.

These figures are drawn from Finolex's published specification sheet for its Flame Retardant (FR) Grade house wire and should be treated as manufacturer-stated values for that specific product line rather than a universal specification for every Finolex 4 sq mm wire.

Finolex 4 Sq mm Wire: Specifications and Selection Factors

Beyond the manufacturer-stated specifications covered above, there are a few practical points worth understanding when evaluating whether a 4 sq mm wire fits a particular electrical application:

  • Electrical load: The connected load on a circuit — in amps or watts — is the starting point for wire sizing. This is typically calculated by an electrician based on the appliances or points on that circuit.
  • Circuit design and protection: The wire size works together with the protective device (such as an MCB) rated for that circuit; both need to be matched to each other and to the load.
  • Installation method: Whether the wire runs through a conduit, is bunched with other cables, or is exposed to open air can affect how it performs thermally, which is why standards specify different conditions for rating cables.
  • Cable length and voltage drop: Longer cable runs can experience a larger voltage drop, which is a separate consideration from current-carrying capacity and can influence what conductor size is appropriate.
  • Ambient conditions: Temperature and ventilation around the cable route can affect its performance and is factored into standard-based cable sizing.
  • Applicable standards: Indian house wiring commonly refers to IS 694 for PVC insulated cables and to the National Electrical Code / relevant wiring rules for installation practice.

Because these factors vary from one installation to another, this article does not state a specific current-carrying capacity for Finolex 4 sq mm wire. Any current rating depends on the exact installation conditions and should be checked against the manufacturer's datasheet for the specific product and against applicable wiring standards, ideally with the input of a qualified electrician.

Finolex 4 Sq mm Wire vs Other Wire Sizes

Understanding how 4 sq mm compares with neighbouring wire sizes can help you judge whether it fits your circuit, without assuming that a bigger number is automatically the "better" choice.

  • 1.5 sq mm: A smaller conductor size generally used for lighter circuits such as lighting points and fan connections, where the load is comparatively low. See Finolex's 1.5 sq mm wire range for reference.
  • 2.5 sq mm: A mid-range size that is commonly used for general power sockets and moderate-load circuits, sitting between 1.5 sq mm and 4 sq mm in typical residential wiring layouts.
  • 4 sq mm: Used where the circuit load is higher than what 1.5 sq mm or 2.5 sq mm wiring is designed for — for example, certain power circuits, sub-mains, or specific higher-load points, subject to proper load calculation.
  • 6 sq mm: A larger size typically considered for circuits with a still higher load, longer cable runs where voltage drop is a concern, or sub-main and distribution wiring. You can compare it directly with the Finolex 6 sq mm wires range.

The right size for your situation depends on the specific circuit's load, length, and design — not simply on choosing the largest available size. Oversizing or undersizing a wire both create their own practical and cost implications, which is why a proper load assessment matters more than a general size preference.
