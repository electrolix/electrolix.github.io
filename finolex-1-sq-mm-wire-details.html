What Is Finolex 1 sq mm Wire?

Finolex 1 sq mm wire is a single-core, PVC-insulated electrical wire with a copper conductor whose nominal cross-sectional area is 1.0 square millimetre. It is manufactured by Finolex Cables Limited, one of India's largest wire and cable manufacturers, and is sold as part of the company's house wiring range under product lines such as FR (Flame Retardant), FRLSH (Flame Retardant Low Smoke Halogen-free), and FinoUltra.

The “1 sq mm” figure describes the conductor size, not the outer diameter of the finished wire. It refers to the combined cross-sectional area of the fine copper strands that make up the conductor. Indian house wiring uses a standard range of these sizes — 0.75, 1.0, 1.5, 2.5, 4.0, and 6.0 sq mm are the most common — and each step up allows the wire to carry more current safely.

Finolex 1 sq mm wire is manufactured in line with IS 694:2010 (with its amendments), the Bureau of Indian Standards specification for PVC-insulated cables used in electrical wiring rated up to 1100 volts. Finolex house wires carry the ISI mark, indicating BIS certification for this standard.

Understanding Wire Insulation Types: FR, FRLS, and FRLSH

Finolex 1 sq mm wire is available in more than one insulation grade, and it's worth understanding the difference before you buy, because the exact product code matters for the application:

  • FR (Flame Retardant): The base-level PVC compound resists the spread of flame and is the most common and economical option for standard residential and light commercial wiring.
  • FRLS (Flame Retardant Low Smoke): Adds reduced smoke emission during a fire, which is useful in spaces with limited ventilation, such as smaller rooms or basements.
  • FRLSH (Flame Retardant Low Smoke Halogen-free): Goes a step further by reducing halogen gas emission as well as smoke, which is relevant in enclosed or densely occupied spaces such as offices, hospitals, and multi-storey residential buildings, where toxic fumes during a fire are a bigger safety concern than the fire itself.

All three are sold under the Finolex 1 sq mm wire range. The right choice depends on the building type, local fire safety norms, and the specific area being wired — for enclosed or high-occupancy spaces, FRLS or FRLSH is generally the safer choice, while standard FR is adequate for typical home wiring in well-ventilated areas.

Applications of Finolex 1 sq mm Wire

Because of its current-carrying capacity of around 11–12 amps, Finolex 1 sq mm wire is best suited to relatively light electrical loads rather than high-draw circuits. According to the manufacturer, its FR PVC range is intended for homes, buildings, and industrial buildings, as well as engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC) type projects.

In practice, this size is most commonly used for:

  • Lighting circuits: wiring for ceiling lights, wall lights, and other fixed lighting points, where current draw is low
  • Switch and control wiring: connections between switches, fans (at lower speeds/loads), and small control panels
  • Internal wiring of light fixtures and low-load points: short internal runs within panels, switchboards, and junction boxes
  • General-purpose domestic and light industrial wiring: wherever the connected load stays within the wire's rated capacity

It is not intended for high-current applications such as power sockets for heavy appliances, air conditioners, water heaters, or kitchen equipment — these draw considerably more current and require a larger conductor size. For any circuit carrying variable, high, or unclear loads, it's best to have a licensed electrician calculate the load and confirm the correct wire size, rather than relying on general guidance alone.

A few practical takeaways from this comparison:

  • 1 sq mm vs 0.75 sq mm: The 1 sq mm wire carries noticeably more current than the 0.75 sq mm size, making it the more common choice for standard lighting and switch wiring, while 0.75 sq mm is typically reserved for very light-duty or signal-type wiring.
  • 1 sq mm vs 1.5 sq mm: The step up to 1.5 sq mm adds meaningful headroom — useful where you're running longer cable lengths, feeding multiple light points from one circuit, or want a safety margin. Many electricians use 1.5 sq mm as the default for general lighting circuits precisely for this reason, reserving 1 sq mm for shorter or lighter-duty runs.
  • 1 sq mm vs 4 sq mm or 6 sq mm: These larger sizes are meant for power circuits and higher-load applications, not lighting. They are not interchangeable with 1 sq mm wire for the same circuit.

No single size is “better” in absolute terms — the right choice depends on the connected load, the length of the run, and applicable wiring codes. If you're unsure which size fits your circuit, it's worth reviewing options like the Finolex 1.5 sq mm wire or Finolex 0.75 sq mm wire for comparison, or consulting an electrician for a load calculation.

Copper Conductor Quality

The conductor is the part of the wire that actually carries electricity, so its quality directly affects performance. Finolex's technical literature states that its FR PVC wires use 99.96% pure, electrolytic-grade, bright annealed bare copper with a minimum conductivity of 100% IACS. Higher conductor purity generally means lower resistive losses and more consistent performance over the life of the installation, though actual performance in a given installation still depends on correct sizing, connections, and installation practice.

Finolex manufactures its copper conductors in-house at its Goa facility, which the company presents as a way of maintaining consistent quality across its wire range. You can read more about the company's copper wire manufacturing on the Finolex copper wires page.

Factors That Affect Finolex 1 sq mm Wire Price

The price of Finolex 1 sq mm wire is not fixed — it varies based on several factors, so this guide does not quote a fixed figure that could quickly become outdated. Instead, here are the main factors that influence what you'll pay:

  • Insulation variant: FR, FRLS, and FRLSH grades are priced differently, with more advanced fire-safety insulation (like FRLSH) typically costing more than standard FR.
  • Copper market rates: Copper is a globally traded commodity, and its price fluctuates. Since copper is the main raw material in this wire, changes in copper prices affect the final selling price.
  • Coil length and quantity: Buying a full 90-metre coil versus a cut length, or ordering in bulk versus retail quantity, changes the per-metre and per-coil cost.
  • Seller and channel: Prices can differ between authorised dealers, online marketplaces, and retail electrical shops, partly due to differing margins and partly due to regional distribution costs.
  • Taxes and shipping: GST and any applicable delivery charges are added on top of the base product price and vary by order value and location.

How to Buy Finolex 1 sq mm Wire Online

Buying electrical wire online is convenient, but a few checks help make sure you get the right product for your circuit:

  1. Confirm the exact conductor size. Make sure the listing clearly states “1 sq mm” or “1.0 sq mm” and matches the current rating you need for your circuit.
  2. Check the insulation type. Decide whether you need standard FR, or the added smoke/halogen protection of FRLS or FRLSH, based on where the wire will be installed.
  3. Verify the ISI mark and BIS compliance. Genuine Finolex house wires are ISI marked and manufactured to IS 694:2010. Check that the listing and product images show this clearly.
  4. Check the coil length and quantity. Confirm whether you're ordering a full 90-metre coil or a cut length, and how many coils or metres you actually need for the job.
  5. Buy from an authorised or reputable seller. This reduces the risk of counterfeit or substandard cable, which is a genuine concern in the wire and cable market.
  6. Ask about warranty only if it's explicitly stated. Don't assume warranty terms that aren't clearly mentioned on the product listing.
  7. Match the wire to the actual load. If you're unsure whether 1 sq mm is sufficient for your circuit, check the current ratings in this guide or consult an electrician before ordering.

If your project involves broader house wiring rather than a single circuit, it may help to look at Finolex's house wiring cables for domestic and commercial use or the Finolex wires for lighting range, both of which cover related sizes and applications you may need alongside the 1 sq mm wire.
