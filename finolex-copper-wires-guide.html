Types of Finolex Copper Wires

Finolex does not make a single, one-size-fits-all wire. Instead, the brand offers several types, each designed for a specific safety requirement or budget. Here are the main ones you should know about.

FR (Flame Retardant) Wires

Finolex FR wires are insulated with a special PVC compound that resists catching fire easily. If there is a short circuit or a spark nearby, FR-insulated wires slow down the spread of flames. This makes them a good baseline choice for homes and small offices where basic fire safety is a priority.

FRLS (Flame Retardant Low Smoke) Wires

Flame Retardant Low Smoke wires take safety one step further. Along with resisting flames, they release less smoke during a fire. This matters a lot, because smoke inhalation is one of the leading causes of injury and death during indoor fires, not the flames themselves. If you are wiring an apartment, a shop, or any space where people gather, Finolex FRLS wires are usually the smarter option.

FRLSH (Flame Retardant Low Smoke Halogen-Free) Wires

For even higher safety standards, Finolex offers FRLSH wires. These combine flame retardance and low smoke output with a halogen-free composition, which means fewer toxic and corrosive gases are released in case of fire. Commercial buildings, high-rise apartments, hospitals, and data centres often specify FRLSH wiring for exactly this reason.

Gold and Silver Range

Finolex also offers Gold and Silver series wires, positioned as premium and value options within its house wiring range. Both are manufactured to the same quality standards, so you can pick a range that suits your project's budget without compromising on core safety and durability.

Choosing between these types usually comes down to your building type and your safety needs. A small residential room may do fine with a basic FR wire, while a commercial complex should ideally use FRLS or FRLSH wiring.

Here is a quick side-by-side comparison to help you decide:

Wire Sizes You Can Choose From

Wire size, measured in square millimetres (sq mm), decides how much electrical load a wire can safely carry. Using an undersized wire for a heavy appliance can cause overheating, so getting the size right is just as important as choosing the right type.

Here is a quick breakdown of common sizes and where they are typically used:

Wire Size Typically Used For
0.75 sq mm Light electrical points, such as ceiling lights and small indicator circuits
1 sq mm Lighting circuits and low-load switch points
1.5 sq mm General lighting and fan connections in homes
4 sq mm Power sockets and moderate-load circuits, including small kitchen appliances
6 sq mm Higher-load circuits such as air conditioners, geysers, and main distribution lines

If you are unsure which size your project needs, it always helps to consult a licensed electrician. Using the correct gauge not only keeps your wiring safe but also helps your electrical system run more efficiently over the years.

How to Spot Genuine Finolex Wires

Because Finolex is such a trusted name, the market unfortunately also has counterfeit and low-grade wires sold under the same brand name. Here is what to check before you buy:

  1. Check the printing on the wire. Genuine Finolex wires have clear, consistent printing along the insulation, showing the brand name, wire size, and voltage grade.
  2. Look for ISI certification marks. Authentic Finolex wires comply with relevant Indian Standards (IS) for PVC insulated cables.
  3. Buy from an authorised seller. This is the simplest way to avoid duplicates. Always purchase from a dealer or online store that sources directly from Finolex or its authorised distributors.
  4. Compare pricing carefully. If a deal looks far cheaper than the usual market rate for genuine Finolex copper wire, it is worth double-checking before you buy.
