What Is a Finolex Wire for Lighting?

Finolex doesn't sell a single product labelled “lighting wire.” Instead, lighting points in Indian homes and offices are wired using Finolex's PVC-insulated single-core house wiring range — the same family used for switches, sockets, and small appliance circuits, just in the conductor sizes suited to lower-current lighting loads. You can browse the full Finolex wires and cables range to see how the lighting-suitable sizes fit into Finolex's broader house wiring lineup. These wires are manufactured with electrolytic-grade copper conductors and PVC insulation, and they're sold under grades such as FR (Flame Retardant), FRLS (Flame Retardant Low Smoke), and FRLSH (Flame Retardant Low Smoke Halogen-free).

It's worth noting that Finolex also sells finished LED lights and luminaires under a separate “Lights” range. That's a different product category — this article is about the copper wiring that carries power to your light points, not the fittings themselves.

Key Specifications and Features

The Finolex house wiring cables used for lighting circuits share a common technical foundation:

  • Conductor: Multi-strand, high-conductivity electrolytic copper. Finolex states each wire is manufactured using more than 99.97% pure electrolytic grade, bright annealed bare copper with more than 100% conductivity.
  • Insulation: PVC, available in FR, FRLS, and FRLSH grades depending on the fire-safety requirement of the installation.
  • Standard compliance: Finolex's FR-grade house wires are described as REACH-compliant PVC flexible cables up to 1.1kV grade, manufactured as per IS 694:2010, the Indian Standard covering PVC-insulated cables for power and lighting wiring.
  • Certification: ISI marking under the BIS Compulsory Registration Scheme, which is mandatory for PVC-insulated cables sold under IS 694:2010 in India.

On tested FR-grade wires, Finolex publishes the following performance figures: minimum 100% IACS conductivity, minimum 150 minutes thermal stability, minimum volume resistivity of 5.5×10¹³ Ω-cm/km, an oxygen index above 29%, and a temperature index of at least 250°C at 21% oxygen. These figures relate specifically to the FR-grade variant tested by Finolex; other grades (FRLS, FRLSH) may carry different published figures, so always check the datasheet for the specific product and size you're buying.

Choosing the Right Wire Size for Lighting Circuits

Lighting points draw comparatively small current — a handful of amps per point — so lighting circuits are typically wired with smaller conductor sizes than power sockets or appliance circuits, which need to handle higher sustained loads.

Conductor Size Typical Use
0.75 sq mm Light control wiring, low-load indicator circuits
1.0 sq mm A common choice of Finolex wire for lighting points and switch wiring in homes
1.5 sq mm Lighting circuits and general-purpose wiring, including runs with multiple light points on one circuit
2.5 sq mm and above Typically reserved for power sockets and higher-load appliance circuits, not lighting

Finolex 1 sq mm wires handle higher loads compared to the 0.75 sq mm variant, making them a preferred choice for residential wiring, lighting circuits, and general electrical installations. The exact size you need depends on the length of the run, the number of light points on that circuit, and the load calculation done by your electrician — this is where a qualified professional's input matters more than a generic size recommendation.

Actual current-carrying capacity for a given size also depends on the installation method (in conduit, bunched, in free air) and ambient temperature, as set out in the relevant wiring standards. Don't rely on a single number without checking the specific product's rating and your installation conditions.

FR vs FRLS vs FRLSH: Which Insulation Grade Do You Need?

This is one of the most common points of confusion when picking a Finolex cable for lighting, so it's worth breaking down clearly:

  • FR (Flame Retardant): The insulation resists the spread of flame, meaning the cable won't easily sustain or propagate a fire even if it's directly exposed to one. This is the baseline safety grade for modern Indian house wiring.
  • FRLS (Flame Retardant Low Smoke): Adds reduced smoke emission on top of flame retardance — useful in enclosed spaces like apartments and offices where smoke inhalation, not just fire spread, is a safety concern.
  • FRLSH (Flame Retardant Low Smoke Halogen-free): Goes a step further by eliminating halogen compounds from the insulation, which reduces the release of toxic and corrosive gases if the cable does burn. This grade is often specified in hospitals, malls, high-rises, and other high-occupancy buildings.

For a typical home, FR or FRLS is usually sufficient. For commercial buildings, multi-storey residential complexes, or spaces with stricter fire-safety codes, FRLSH is often the safer and sometimes mandated choice. If you're unsure which grade your project requires, check with your electrical consultant or the building's fire-safety guidelines rather than assuming.

Applications and Where These Wires Are Used

Finolex single-core wires in the smaller conductor sizes are commonly used for lighting and light-appliance wiring in homes and offices, and also see use in industrial settings for low-voltage control wiring. Typical applications for Finolex wire lighting circuits include:

  • Ceiling and wall light points in residential wiring
  • Switch and switchboard wiring
  • False-ceiling and concealed conduit wiring for lighting
  • Light control circuits in offices and commercial spaces
  • Low-load control wiring in panels and industrial installations

These wires are not intended for high-load circuits such as air conditioners, geysers, or heavy motor loads — those applications need larger conductor sizes and, in many cases, different cable constructions altogether. Always match the wire to the actual load; undersizing a conductor for its circuit is a genuine fire risk, not just a technical inefficiency.

A residential lighting circuit with a handful of points on a short run may only need 1.0 sq mm FR wire, while a longer commercial lighting circuit in a building with fire-code requirements may call for 1.5 sq mm FRLSH.

Understanding Pricing

Prices for Finolex wire for lighting vary by several factors, so there's no single “market rate” that applies across the board:

  • Conductor size: larger cross-sections use more copper and cost more per metre
  • Insulation grade: FRLSH typically costs more than FR due to the added halogen-free compound
  • Reel length and quantity: bulk or full-reel purchases are usually more cost-effective per metre than small cut lengths
  • Copper market rates: copper is a globally traded commodity, so wire prices move with copper prices
  • Seller and GST: authorised distributors apply GST-compliant pricing, and rates can vary between sellers

Because copper prices fluctuate, always check current pricing directly on the product page rather than relying on a figure from an article or price list that may be outdated.
