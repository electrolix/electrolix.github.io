What Type of Finolex Wire Is Used for Power Sockets?

Finolex's domestic and commercial wiring range includes PVC-insulated single-core wires used for house wiring — this covers socket or plug-point circuits, lighting circuits, and general internal power wiring. These wires use bright annealed, electrolytic-grade bare copper conductors, and Finolex offers them in flame-retardant (FR), flame-retardant low-smoke (FRLS) and flame-retardant low-smoke halogen (FRLSH) PVC insulation variants. They are manufactured to conform to IS 694, the Indian Standard that governs PVC-insulated cables and flexible cords for voltages up to and including 1100V.

Because Finolex offers this wiring across several conductor sizes and insulation grades, the exact product used for a socket point on one project may differ from another, depending on the circuit's expected load, cable run length and the electrical design followed by the installer. Why Wire Selection Matters for Power Socket Circuits

A socket circuit's wire has to carry the current drawn by whatever is plugged in — without overheating, without an unacceptable voltage drop, and without compromising the insulation's fire performance. An undersized conductor run under a heavier load than it was designed for can heat up, degrade its insulation over time, and increase fire risk. An oversized conductor is not "unsafe" as such, but it adds unnecessary cost and can make terminations harder.

This is why picking the right conductor size, insulation type and voltage rating is not a cosmetic decision — it is a core part of getting a power-socket circuit right, alongside correct circuit protection (MCBs/RCCBs), proper earthing and competent installation.

Key Specifications to Check Before Choosing a Finolex Wire for Power Sockets

Before shortlisting a specific Finolex product for socket wiring, it is worth checking the following specifications against your circuit's requirements and, where needed, an electrician's or engineer's recommendation.

Conductor Size

Conductor cross-section (measured in sq.mm) is the single biggest factor in how much current a wire can carry. Indian domestic and commercial socket wiring commonly uses sizes in the 1.5 sq.mm to 6 sq.mm range, with the exact size depending on the circuit's design current, the number of sockets on that circuit, cable length, installation method and the protective device rating. There is no single conductor size that is correct for every socket circuit — it should be selected as part of the electrical design, not assumed from a general rule of thumb.

Conductor Material

Finolex house-wiring cables use bare bright annealed electrolytic-grade copper conductors, which offer high conductivity and are the standard conductor material for domestic and commercial socket and lighting wiring in India. Copper's low resistance per unit length helps limit heat generation and voltage drop compared with lower-conductivity materials.

Insulation

Finolex offers plain FR (flame retardant), FRLS (flame retardant low smoke) and FRLSH (flame retardant low smoke halogen) PVC insulation on its house-wiring range. FR insulation resists the spread of fire; FRLS and FRLSH grades additionally reduce smoke density and, in the case of FRLSH, halogen gas emission during a fire, which can matter in enclosed spaces such as offices, hospitals or high-occupancy buildings. The right insulation grade depends on the building type, local fire-safety requirements and the installer's or consultant's specification.

Voltage Rating

Finolex's PVC-insulated house-wiring cables manufactured to IS 694 are rated for use up to and including 1100V, which covers standard low-voltage domestic and commercial power distribution, including socket circuits. This is a general voltage grade for the cable construction and should not be read as a guarantee of suitability for every non-standard or industrial application.

Applicable Standards

Finolex's PVC-insulated house-wiring cables are manufactured to conform to IS 694, the Bureau of Indian Standards specification for PVC-insulated cables and flexible cords for rated voltages up to 1100V. Products carrying the ISI mark have been tested against this standard's requirements. Because certifications and exact standard revisions can vary by product series and size, always confirm the applicable standard and certification marking on the specific product listing or datasheet before purchase.

How to Choose Finolex Wire for Different Power-Socket Applications

Different sockets in a building rarely carry the same load. A socket meant for a phone charger or a lamp draws far less current than one feeding a water heater, air conditioner, or kitchen appliance on a dedicated circuit. Because of this, the right wire for a general-purpose 6A socket circuit will often differ from the wire needed on a 16A or higher-rated socket meant for heavier appliances.

A practical approach is to group sockets by expected load, confirm the circuit's design current and protective device rating with a qualified electrician, and then select the conductor size and insulation grade accordingly — rather than choosing a wire size first and assuming the load will fit. This is also why product listings and buyer searches for a power socket Finolex wire usually specify a size (such as 1.5 sq.mm or 2.5 sq.mm) rather than relying on the brand name alone; the size, not just the brand, is what determines suitability for a given socket circuit.

Finolex Wire vs Finolex Cable for Power Socket Applications

"Wire" and "cable" are often used interchangeably in everyday conversation, but there is a technical distinction worth knowing when you are comparing products. A wire generally refers to a single, insulated conductor. A cable typically refers to an assembly of one or more insulated conductors, sometimes with an additional outer sheath, used together as a single unit.

In practice, when someone searches for this kind of product for socket wiring, they are usually referring to the same family of PVC-insulated house-wiring products described above — Finolex's own product literature and retail listings use "wire" and "cable" somewhat interchangeably for this single-core, socket-and-lighting-wiring range. The distinction becomes more relevant with multicore or sheathed constructions, which are built and used differently from single-core house-wiring conductors. If a specific job calls for a multicore or armoured cable rather than single-core wiring, that should be confirmed separately, since it is a different product category with its own specifications.

Factors That Affect the Price of Finolex Wires

Finolex wire and cable pricing is influenced by several factors rather than a single fixed rate:

  • Conductor size — larger cross-sections use more copper and typically cost more per metre.
  • Product series and insulation grade — FRLSH-insulated products generally cost more than plain FR versions due to the added compound and processing.
  • Coil or drum length — Finolex house-wiring cables are commonly sold in 90-metre coils, and per-unit pricing can differ from bulk drum lengths.
  • Quantity purchased — bulk or project-quantity orders may be priced differently from single-coil retail purchases.
  • Copper market prices — since copper is a globally traded commodity, its price movements directly affect cable costs over time.
  • Seller, applicable taxes and shipping — final delivered price varies by seller and location.

Because pricing changes with market conditions and varies by seller, this article does not quote a specific price. Check current pricing directly on the product page before purchase.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before Purchasing

Before you buy Finolex wiring for your socket circuits, verify the following:

  1. Exact product and series — confirm the specific Finolex product name and series, not just the brand.
  2. Conductor size — match it to your circuit's design current, ideally confirmed by an electrician.
  3. Conductor material and construction — confirm bare bright annealed copper and single-core or multicore construction as required.
  4. Insulation type — FR, FRLS or FRLSH, based on the building's fire-safety needs.
  5. Voltage rating — confirm it matches your circuit's requirements (commonly 1100V grade for domestic/commercial socket wiring).
  6. Applicable standard and certification — check for IS 694 conformance and ISI marking where relevant.
  7. Required length and quantity — coils are commonly available in 90-metre lengths; calculate total requirement with some allowance for wastage.
  8. Colour coding — Finolex house-wiring cables are available in standard colours (red, yellow, blue, black, green) used for phase, neutral and earth identification as per wiring practice.
  9. Seller, current price, availability and applicable taxes/shipping — confirm all of these at the time of purchase, since they change over time.
  10. Manufacturer documentation — refer to the product datasheet for anything not covered on the retail listing.
