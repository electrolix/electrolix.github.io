What Are Finolex FR Wires?

FR stands for Flame Retardant. These are PVC-insulated electrical wires manufactured to slow down the spread of fire along the length of the cable if it comes into contact with flame or excessive heat. In India, this category of wire falls under IS 694:2010, the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) specification for PVC-insulated cables and cords used for electric power and lighting at working voltages up to 1100 V. IS 694:2010 specifically defines FR (flame retardant) and FR-LSH (flame retardant, low smoke and halogen) as recognised fire-performance categories.

Key Features of Finolex FR Wires

While exact specifications can vary by wire size and variant, Finolex FR wires generally share these characteristics:

  • Flame-retardant PVC insulation: The insulation compound is formulated to have a higher oxygen index (the amount of oxygen needed to sustain combustion) and a higher temperature index than standard PVC, which helps resist flame propagation along the wire.
  • Copper conductor: Finolex states that its wires use high-purity electrolytic-grade bare copper conductors, which support good electrical conductivity. As with any manufacturer specification, it's worth confirming the exact grade for the specific product you're buying.
  • 1100 V voltage grade: Most Finolex FR house wires are rated for a working voltage of 1100 V, which is the standard grade used in Indian residential and light commercial wiring under IS 694.
  • ISI marking: Wires manufactured to IS 694 and carrying the ISI mark have undergone BIS-mandated testing, including flammability, insulation resistance, and high-voltage spark tests.
  • Colour-coded insulation: FR wires are typically available in standard identification colours such as red, black, blue, green, and yellow, which helps electricians follow consistent wiring practices.

A note on accuracy: specifications such as insulation thickness, overall diameter, and current-carrying capacity differ across wire sizes (for example, 1 sq mm versus 6 sq mm) and between product variants. Always refer to the datasheet or the FR wire Finolex product page for the specific size and variant you intend to buy, rather than assuming one specification applies across the board.

How FR Wires Help Improve Electrical Safety

Ordinary PVC insulation can catch fire relatively easily and, once it does, can help flames travel along the length of the cable. FR-grade PVC compounds are designed to resist this flame propagation, which is why FR wires are now the default choice for domestic and commercial wiring in India rather than plain, non-FR PVC wire.

That said, it's important to be clear about what FR wires can and can't do. Flame-retardant insulation reduces the risk of a fire spreading along the wiring; it does not make a home or building fireproof, and it is not a substitute for other essential safety practices, such as:

  • Using correctly rated MCBs, fuses, and circuit breakers for each circuit
  • Avoiding overloading of sockets and circuits
  • Getting wiring installed and periodically inspected by a licensed electrician
  • Following the applicable Indian electrical safety codes and local regulations

If you're unsure about circuit design, wire sizing, or load calculations for your home or workplace, it's best to consult a qualified electrician rather than rely solely on the wire's FR rating.

FR vs FR-LSH: What's the Difference?

You'll often see related terms like FRLS and FR-LSH alongside FR wires, and it helps to understand how they differ:

  • FR (Flame Retardant): Insulation resists flame propagation. This is the base fire-performance category under IS 694.
  • FR-LSH (Flame Retardant, Low Smoke and Halogen): In addition to flame retardance, this category is formulated to reduce the smoke and halogen (toxic gas) emitted if the insulation does burn. This matters because, in an enclosed space, smoke and toxic fumes are often a bigger hazard than the fire itself.

Because reduced smoke and toxic emission can matter more in densely occupied or enclosed spaces, FR-LSH wires are frequently recommended for offices, multi-storey residential buildings, hospitals, and other spaces with higher occupancy or restricted ventilation. For a typical independent home with normal ventilation, standard FR wire is generally considered adequate, though your electrician is best placed to advise based on the specific building and local code requirements.

Where Finolex FR Wires Are Used

Finolex FR wires are designed for general power and lighting applications at 1100 V, which covers most everyday electrical wiring needs, including:

  • Residential house wiring: Circuits for lighting points, switches, and power sockets in apartments and independent homes.
  • Commercial spaces: Offices, shops, and other commercial establishments that need reliable, code-compliant wiring.

  • Power socket circuits: Higher cross-section FR wires are used where appliances draw more current, such as sockets for geysers, air conditioners, or kitchen appliances.
  • Light industrial and control wiring: FR wires also find use in panel wiring and industrial control applications where flame retardance is required.

Available Wire Sizes

Finolex FR wires are available in a range of cross-sectional sizes (measured in square millimetres, or sq mm), each suited to different load requirements. Smaller sizes are typically used for lighting and low-load circuits, while larger sizes handle higher-current appliance circuits. Commonly available sizes include:

  • 0.75 sq mm — often used for low-load and control wiring
  • 1 sq mm — commonly used for lighting circuits
  • 1.5 sq mm — a frequently used size for lighting and general-purpose points
  • 4 sq mm — suited to higher-load socket circuits
  • 6 sq mm — used for heavier appliance loads and sub-mains

The exact current-carrying capacity of each size depends on several factors, including ambient temperature, the number of wires bunched together, and installation method (in open air versus enclosed conduit). Rather than relying on a generic number, it's best to check the manufacturer's datasheet for the specific product or ask a qualified electrician to calculate the right size for your load.

How to Choose the Right Finolex FR Wire

A few practical factors can help narrow down the right choice:

  • Type of circuit: Lighting circuits generally need smaller sizes than power socket or appliance circuits.
  • Connected load: Higher-wattage appliances such as geysers, air conditioners, and induction cooktops need a wire size that can safely carry the current they draw.
  • Wiring length: Longer cable runs can experience voltage drop, which may call for a larger cross-section even for a moderate load.
  • Building type and occupancy: As covered above, densely occupied or enclosed buildings may call for FR-LSH rather than standard FR wire.
  • New wiring versus rewiring: If you're replacing old wiring, it's a good opportunity to also review whether your existing circuit sizing still matches your current appliance load.

If you'd rather buy a full 90-metre coil for a complete wiring project instead of shorter lengths, Finolex's coils are built with the same high-conductivity copper conductor used across its house wiring range.

Finolex FR Wire Price: What Affects It

Finolex FR wire prices are not fixed and can change based on several factors, so it's worth understanding what typically drives the price rather than expecting a single flat rate across sizes and sellers:

  • Wire size: Larger cross-sections use more copper and cost more per metre than smaller sizes.
  • Copper price fluctuations: Since copper is a globally traded commodity, its price movements directly affect wire pricing across all brands, not just Finolex.
  • Coil length: A full 90-metre coil is usually more cost-effective per metre than buying shorter cut lengths.
  • Variant (FR, FR-LSH, Gold, Silver): Different fire-performance categories and product grades are priced differently based on the materials and processing involved.
  • Seller and location: Prices can vary between retailers, distributors, and regions.

Because pricing changes frequently, it's best to check the current FR wire Finolex price and price list directly on a retailer's product page rather than relying on a number that may already be outdated by the time you read it.
