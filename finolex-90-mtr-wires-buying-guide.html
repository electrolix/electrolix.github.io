What Are Finolex 90 Mtr Wires?

Finolex 90 mtr wires are single-core, PVC insulated wires. They come in a coil of 90 meters. This length is a standard size across the wire industry in India. It is not just a Finolex thing. Most top brands follow this format.

Each coil uses a copper conductor. It is wrapped in PVC insulation. This layer protects the wire from heat, moisture, and daily wear.

These wires work well for home wiring. They also suit lighting points, power sockets, and small industrial jobs. The coil is compact. It is easy to store and carry. There is little to no wastage.

One person can carry a coil with ease. An electrician can take it up a staircase without help. A homeowner doing a small repair does not need to buy more wire than needed.

Types of Insulation: FR, FRLS, and FRLSH

Not all Finolex 90 mtr wires use the same insulation. Your choice depends on where you plan to install the wire.

  • FR (Flame Retardant) wires slow down fire along the wire. They work well for most homes.
  • FRLS (Flame Retardant Low Smoke) wires go a step further. They cut down smoke during a fire too. This helps in closed spaces, where clear visibility matters most in an emergency.
  • FRLSH (Flame Retardant Low Smoke Halogen-free) wires cut down halogen gas release. These suit hospitals, malls, and large public buildings.

For a home, FR or FRLS wires work fine. For large commercial buildings, FRLSH is often the safer choice.

Available Sizes in the 90 Mtr Range

Finolex 90 mtr wires come in many sizes. Size is measured in square millimetres, or sq mm. Your choice depends on the load your circuit needs to carry.

  • 0.75 sq mm wires suit low-load jobs. Think doorbells or small indicator lights.
  • 1 sq mm wires and 1.5 sq mm wires are common picks. They work well for lighting points and general home circuits.
  • 4 sq mm wires suit power sockets. They handle appliances that draw more current, like geysers or ACs.
  • 6 sq mm wires handle heavier loads. Use them for sub-mains or short industrial runs.

Size matters for safety. A wire that is too thin can overheat over time. Check your circuit's load before you pick a size. When in doubt, ask a qualified electrician.

Tips for a Safe and Long-Lasting Wiring Job

Buying the right wire is just the first step. How you install and use it matters too. Here are some simple tips to keep in mind:

  • Never overload a circuit. Match the wire size to the appliance load, not the other way round.
  • Avoid sharp bends. Bending a wire too much can damage the insulation over time.
  • Keep wiring away from water and damp areas, unless the wire is rated for such use.
  • Use proper joints and connectors. Loose connections are a common cause of overheating.
  • Get your wiring checked by a licensed electrician, especially for new installations or major repairs.

These steps are simple. But they make a real difference. Good habits, paired with good wire, give you a safer home for years to come.
