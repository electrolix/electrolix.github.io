Finolex 1.5 Sq mm Wire: Specifications, Applications and Price Guide

If you're planning house wiring or replacing a faulty circuit, chances are you've come across the Finolex 1.5 sq mm wire. It's one of the most commonly used wire sizes in Indian homes, mainly because it fits the load requirements of lighting points and small fixtures without being oversized or wasteful.

This guide explains what a Finolex 1.5 sq mm electrical wire actually is, what "1.5 sq mm" means in practical terms, its verified construction and specifications, where it should (and shouldn't) be used, how it compares with nearby wire sizes, and what to check before you buy one online.

What Is Finolex 1.5 Sq mm Wire?

Finolex 1.5 sq mm wire is a single-core, PVC-insulated copper wire manufactured by Finolex Cables Ltd, one of India's largest cable and wire manufacturers. It falls under Finolex's house wiring range and is built around a stranded copper conductor with a nominal cross-sectional area of 1.5 square millimetres.

These wires are sold as flexible, multi-strand copper conductors insulated with PVC compound, and are primarily intended for fixed indoor wiring — the kind that goes inside conduits, walls, and switchboards in homes, offices, and similar low-voltage installations.

On Eleczo, Finolex 1.5 sq mm wires are listed as single-core, flexible copper cables available in multiple insulation colours, sold both as retail lengths and standard rolls, for use in residential, commercial, and light industrial electrical work.

What Does "1.5 Sq mm" Actually Mean?

The number "1.5 sq mm" refers to the nominal cross-sectional area of the copper conductor inside the wire, measured in square millimetres. This is a standard way of specifying wire size in India and most of the world (as opposed to gauge numbers used in some other countries).

In simple terms, a larger cross-sectional area means the conductor can carry more current with less resistance and less heat build-up, while a smaller cross-sectional area is suited to lighter loads. A 1.5 sq mm wire sits just above the 1 sq mm wire, which is used for marginally lighter fixture loads, and below the 2.5 sq mm and 4 sq mm wires commonly used for power sockets and larger appliances.

This is why you'll often see the same product referred to interchangeably as Finolex 1.5 mm wire, 1.5 mm Finolex wire, or Finolex 1.5 sq mm electrical wire in casual usage — technically, though, the correct unit is square millimetres (sq mm) of conductor area, not millimetres of wire diameter.

Conductor and construction

  • Conductor material: annealed, bare (or tinned, depending on variant) electrolytic-grade stranded copper
  • Nominal conductor cross-section: 1.5 sq mm
  • Insulation: PVC compound, in Finolex's Flame Retardant (FR) grade for the variants listed on Eleczo
  • Number of cores: single core (multi-strand, flexible)
  • Applicable standard: IS 694:2010 (Reaffirmed 2015), which governs PVC-insulated cables used in power and lighting installations, including household wiring

Current-carrying capacity

General engineering references based on IS 694 typically place the current-carrying capacity of a single-core, 1.5 sq mm PVC-insulated copper wire in the range of roughly 13 to 18 amps, though the exact figure depends on the installation method (surface wiring, conduit, or bunched with other cables), ambient temperature, and the number of circuits sharing a conduit. This is general standards-based guidance rather than a Finolex-specific published rating, and actual derating should follow the applicable IS 694 tables or advice from a qualified electrician.

Applications and Uses

1.5 sq mm wire is one of the most widely used sizes in Indian residential wiring, mainly because it comfortably handles the electrical load of lighting and small fixture circuits without unnecessary copper cost.

Where it's commonly used

  • Lighting circuits — ceiling lights, tube lights, LED fixtures, and decorative lighting. See Finolex wires for lighting for lighting-specific options.
  • Ceiling fan wiring and fan regulator connections
  • Switch and switchboard internal wiring for low-load points
  • Doorbells, exhaust fans, and other light fixed appliances
  • General domestic wiring in homes, offices, and small commercial spaces where the connected load per point is modest

Where it should generally be avoided

1.5 sq mm wire is not intended for high-load circuits. Industry guidance — including Eleczo's own product information — advises against using 1.5 sq mm wire for heavy-duty appliances such as geysers, air conditioners, and kitchen power sockets, since these draw significantly more current than lighting points. For such loads, thicker wire such as Finolex 4 sq mm wire is typically used instead.

The right wire size for any circuit depends on the connected electrical load, the length of the cable run, and the applicable wiring rules. For any new installation or rewiring project, it's worth consulting a qualified electrician to confirm circuit requirements before finalising wire sizes.

Insulation Variants Available

Finolex's house wiring range is manufactured in more than one insulation grade, and this can vary by specific product listing rather than by size alone. On Eleczo, some Finolex 1.5 sq mm single-core cables are listed with PVC FR (Flame Retardant) insulation — see Finolex FR wires for more on this insulation type. Finolex also manufactures FRLS (Flame Retardant Low Smoke) and FRLSH (Flame Retardant Low Smoke Halogen-free) grades across its house wire range for applications that call for lower smoke emission during a fire. Always check the specific product page to confirm which insulation grade applies to the item you're buying, since this affects both performance and price.
